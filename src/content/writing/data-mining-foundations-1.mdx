## Introduction

The **origin of this series of articles** is a course I taught at the Polytechnic University of Catalonia, Spain, between February 2023 and June 2025. The course was called just "Data Mining" and was taught to students in the last year of their undergraduate degree in Computer Science. The course was a mix of theory and practice. During a few years I was iterating the 
the material I inherited from my colleagues using my students' feedback as imput and their hability to perform as measure of success. This series of articles is **the result of that process**. 

I have tried my best to distill the core ideas that I wish my students had known before their first data mining project, with a special focus on the statistical perspective and mathematical rigor. I've made my best effort to present the material in a clear and accessible way, but I must establish from the beginning that it includes some level of difficulty that is intrinsic to the subject. **I hope you find it useful.**

This is the first post of a series on data mining foundations. The goal of this series is to provide a solid understanding of the core ideas that are often overlooked in introductory courses, but are essential for anyone who wants to do data mining in practice.

## What I plan to cover in this series?

In this series, I will cover the topics I thaugh at university plus the topics I consider required to understand this material. This algorigthms and methods will be focus **only on classification problems**. The topics I plan to cover are the next ones:

* Comparing classifiers. How to know for sure what is the best one
* Feature selection and dimensionality reduction.
* Working with unbalanced datasets. Introduction to undersampling and oversampling.
* Non-parametric methods: Naive Bayes 
* Non-parametric methods: KNN
* Decission trees
* Introduction to Meta Methods
* Introduction to Ensembles
* Introduction to Support Vector Machines
* Conclussions
