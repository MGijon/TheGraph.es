## Comparing classifiers: the statistical perspective

When we compare two classifiers, we are usually interested in their performance on a test set. The most common metric is accuracy, but it is not enough to tell us whether the difference between two classifiers is statistically significant or just due to random chance.

### The hypotheses

If both classifiers have the same error rate, a disagreement is equally likely to go either way:

$$
H_0 : p_b = p_c \qquad \text{vs.} \qquad H_1 : p_b \neq p_c
$$

## The McNemar's statistical test

Suppose we have two classifiers, $A$ and $B$, evaluated on the **same** test set of $n$ examples.
Accuracy alone can't tell us whether the difference between them is real or just noise. Because both
models see the same examples, the observations are _paired_, and McNemar's test is the natural tool.

### The contingency table

For every test example we record whether each classifier got it right:

|                 | $B$ correct | $B$ wrong |
| --------------- | ----------- | --------- |
| **$A$ correct** | $a$         | $b$       |
| **$A$ wrong**   | $c$         | $d$       |

with $a + b + c + d = n$. The cells $a$ and $d$ are examples where both models agree, so they say nothing
about which one is better. Only the _discordant_ cells $b$ and $c$ carry information.



### The statistic

Under $H_0$, and when $b + c$ is not too small, the statistic

$$
\chi^2 = \frac{(b - c)^2}{b + c}
$$

follows approximately a $\chi^2$ distribution with $1$ degree of freedom. A common refinement is
Edwards' continuity correction:

$$
\chi^2_{\text{cc}} = \frac{\left(\lvert b - c \rvert - 1\right)^2}{b + c}
$$

When $b + c < 25$, it is safer to use the exact test: under $H_0$, $b \sim \operatorname{Binomial}(b + c, \tfrac{1}{2})$, so

$$
p = 2 \sum_{k=0}^{\min(b,\,c)} \binom{b + c}{k} \left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{b + c}.
$$

### A worked example. Applying McNemar's test to two classifiers

On a test set of $n = 1000$ examples we observe $b = 40$ (only $A$ is right) and $c = 18$ (only $B$ is right):

$$
\begin{aligned}
\chi^2_{\text{cc}} &= \frac{\left(\lvert 40 - 18 \rvert - 1\right)^2}{40 + 18} \\
                   &= \frac{21^2}{58} \\
                   &\approx 7.60
\end{aligned}
$$

The critical value at $\alpha = 0.05$ is $\chi^2_{1,\,0.95} \approx 3.84$. Since $7.60 > 3.84$
($p \approx 0.006$), we reject $H_0$: the difference between the two classifiers is statistically significant.
